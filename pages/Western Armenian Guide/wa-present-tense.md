## **Lesson Objectives**
* Learn about Western Armenian verb infinitives.

## **Western Armenian Verb Infinitives**
There are a few details and differences when it comes to conjugating verbs in the present tense in Western Armenian. In Eastern Armenian, verb infinitives have two endings: **-ել** or **-ալ**. These are present in Western Armenian as well, but there are two additional group of verb infinitives with the ending **-իլ** and **-ուլ**. Below are some examples:

* Խօսիլ - to speak
* ընել - to do
* ապրիլ - to live
* աշխատիլ - work
* սիրել - to love/like
* տեսնել - to see
* երթալ - to go
* կարդալ - to read
* թողուլ - to allow
* հեղուլ - to shed

{: .note-title }
> Why is the spelling different from Eastern Armenian?
>
> In Eastern Armenian, “to speak” is spelled **Խոսել** while in Western Armenian it is **Խօսիլ**. “To do” is spelled **ընել** in Western Armenian, but in Eastern Armenian it is **անել**.
> This difference between the two dialects is because Western Armenian uses classical orthography or Mashtotsian orthography (Հայերէնի դասական ուղղագրութիւն). Most of Eastern Armenian uses reformed orthography which was a result of the Armenian orthography reform in 1920s Soviet Armenia.

## **Conjugating Verbs in Present Tense**

Now the major differences show up here when conjugating present tense in Western Armenian. This is the formula that applies when conjugating present tense:

<div style="text-align: center; font-size: 2em; font-weight: bold;">

կը + [conjugated verb]

</div>

The կը is a particle and it is placed before the conjugated verb. Remember the 4 groups of verb infinitives we covered above? 3 of those cases are conjugated differently from each other while the **-ուլ** group has an interested circumstance covered below. There are also a few exceptions which will be covered as well.

### First Group Verbs: -ել
{: style="margin: 0 !important; line-height: normal !important; font-size: 1.3em !important; font-weight: bold !important;" }

</div>

By following the formula shown above, conjugating -ել verbs happens by writing the Կը particle, then dropping the -ել ending of the verb, and replacing it with **-եմ**, **-ես**, **-է**, **-ենք**, **-էք**, or **-են** based on the person. Here is an example with the verb *սիրել (to love/like)*.


|            | Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
| ---------- | ---------| ------------| ------ | ----------- |
| 1st Person | Ես կը սիր**եմ**|I like | Մենք կը սիր**ենք** | We like|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 2nd Person | Դուն կը սիր**ես** | You (inf., sing.) like | Դուք կը սիր**էք** | You (form., pl.) like|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 3rd Person | Ան or Ինք կը սիր**է** | He/She/It likes | Անոնք or Իրենք կը սիր**են**  | They like |
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|

<br>

### Second Group Verbs: -ալ
{: style="margin: 0 !important; line-height: normal !important; font-size: 1.3em !important; font-weight: bold !important;" }

</div>

Similarly to the first group, conjugating -ալ verbs happens by writing the Կը particle, then dropping the -ալ ending of the verb, but replacing it with **-ամ**, **-աս**, **-այ**, **-անք**, **-աք**, or **-ան** based on the person. Here is an example with the verb *կարդալ (to read)*.


|            | Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
| ---------- | ---------| ------------| ------ | ----------- |
| 1st Person | Ես կը կարդ**ամ** |I read | Մենք կը կարդ**անք** | We read|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 2nd Person | Դուն կը կարդ**աս** | You (inf., sing.) read | Դուք կը կարդ**աք**| You (form., pl.) read|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 3rd Person | Ան or Ինք կը կարդ**այ** | He/She/It reads | Անոնք or Իրենք կը կարդ**ան**  | They read |
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------| 

<br>
<br>


<div markdown="1" style="border: 2px solid #e67e22; background-color: #fff3e0; padding: 10px; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block;">

### Third Group Verbs: -իլ
{: style="margin: 0 !important; line-height: normal !important; font-size: 1.3em !important; font-weight: bold !important;" }

</div>

Similarly to the other two groups, conjugating -իլ verbs happens by writing the Կը particle, then dropping the -իլ ending of the verb, but replacing it with **-իմ**, **-իս**, **-ի**, **-ինք**, **-իք**, or **-ին** based on the person. Here is an example with the verb *խօսիլ (to speak)*.

|            | Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
| ---------- | ---------| ------------| ------ | ----------- |
| 1st Person | Ես կը խօս**իմ** |I speak | Մենք կը խօս**ինք** | We speak|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 2nd Person | Դուն կը խօս**իս** | You (inf., sing.) speak | Դուք կը խօս**իք**| You (form., pl.) speak|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 3rd Person | Ան or Ինք կը խօս**ի**| He/She/It speaks | Անոնք or Իրենք կը խօս**ին**  | They speak |
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------| 

<br>
<br>

<div markdown="1" style="border: 2px solid #e67e22; background-color: #fff3e0; padding: 10px; border-radius: 4px; display: inline-block;">

### Fourth Group Verbs: -ուլ
{: style="margin: 0 !important; line-height: normal !important; font-size: 1.3em !important; font-weight: bold !important;" }

</div>

The fourth group of verbs has the suffix **–ուլ** and it is the rarest type of infinitive, only occurring in a few verbs. The -ուլ infinitives are mainly used in formal conversation, and most of the **-ուլ** infinitives have an equivalent in another word group. For example, the most common **–ուլ** verb in conversation is թողուլ, meaning “to leave; to let, allow" which is from Old/Classical Armenian and it's modern variant is *թողնել* and so you would conjugate this verb in present tense according to -ել group conjugation rules covered above. But, there is a Classical Armenian conjugation rule and it is as follows.

Once again, you write your Կը particle, drop your –ուլ ending, and then replace it with **ում**, **ուս**, **ու**, **ունք**, **ուք**, and **ուն** depending on the person.

|            | Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
| ---------- | ---------| ------------| ------ | ----------- |
| 1st Person | Ես կը թող**ում** |I allow | Մենք կը թող**ունք** | We allow|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 2nd Person | Դուն կը թող**ուս** | You (inf., sing.) allow | Դուք կը թող**ուք**| You (form., pl.) allow|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 3rd Person | Ան or Ինք կը թող**ու**| He/She/It allows | Անոնք or Իրենք կը թող**ուն**  | They allow |
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|

### Special Case for Verbs Starting with a Vowel
{: style="margin: 0 !important; line-height: normal !important; font-size: 1.3em !important; font-weight: bold !important;" }

</div>

If a verb starts with a vowel such as ա, ը, ե, etc. then the ը in the particle կը gets dropped and is replaced with an abatarts (ապաթարց). The formula changes into the following:

<div style="text-align: center; font-size: 2em; font-weight: bold;">

կ՚[conjugated verb]

</div>

Below are two examples with the verbs ընել and աշխատիլ.

|            | Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
| ---------- | ---------| ------------| ------ | ----------- |
| 1st Person | Ես կ՚ըն**եմ** |I do | Մենք կ՚ըն**ենք**| We do |
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 2nd Person | Դուն կ՚ըն**ես** | You (inf., sing.) do | Դուք կ՚ըն**եք**| You (form., pl.) do|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 3rd Person | Ան or Ինք կ՚ըն**է**| He/She/It does | Անոնք or Իրենք կ՚ըն**են** | They do |
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|

### The Verbs գալ, լալ, and տալ
{: style="margin: 0 !important; line-height: normal !important; font-size: 1.3em !important; font-weight: bold !important;" }

</div>

The verbs գալ (to come), լալ (to cry), and տալ (to give) are conjugated slightly differently than other verbs in Western Armenian. Instead of taking the particle կը, they take the particle կու for both the present and imperfect tenses. The endings of the conjugations will be the same as the second group of verbs (**-ալ**). The following is the conjugation of գալ in the present tense and the imperfect (aka past continuous) tense will be covered in the notes for [past continuous](/armenian-notes/pages/Western%20Armenian%20Guide/wa-past-continuous-tense.html).

|            | Singular | Translation | Plural | Translation |
| ---------- | ---------| ------------| ------ | ----------- |
| 1st Person | Ես կը **կու** գամ |I come | Մենք **կու** գանք| We come|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 2nd Person | Դուն **կու** գաս | You (inf., sing.) come | Դուք **կու** գաք| You (form., pl.) come|
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------|
| 3rd Person | Ան or Ինք **կու** գայ| He/She/It comes | Անոնք or Իրենք **կու** գան | They come |
| ---------- | --------------| ---------| --------------| ---------| 


{: .important-title }
> Important
>
> If you have seen my Eastern Armenian notes for the Present Tense, you noticed that I put two different English translations for each case but I did not do that for Western Armenian. Formally and in writing, the above conjugations could mean you are doing something in the present moment or you just do something in general which is the case in Eastern Armenian. In speech, you might hear someone append the suffix **կոր** after the verb + auxiliary to indicate present continuous tense (ex. «ես կ'աշխատիմ-կոր/I am working»). More on this down below.

## **The Controversial Suffix: կոր**

I originally had the two English translations for each of the cases until I found out about the suffix **կոր** and how its purpose is to distinguish between present simple and present continuous tense but only in speech and never in writing (to my knowledge). However, the usage of this suffix is highly controversial for a few reasons. The most significant reason being that it is said to have been borrowed from the Turkish suffix -iyor as the semantic function of **կոր** is very similar to it which is the Turkish form of present continuous tense (Donabedian, 2018, p. 23).

But interestingly enough, an alternative origin story of the suffix **կոր** might be, as Donabedian (2018) says, a reduplication of **Կա** (which means "there is"). What do you mean "reduplication"? Well, the particle **կը** that we covered above is likely derived from **Կա** as well. In Eastern Armenian, adding **կ-** before your verb is a marker of future tense. As you can derive from this instance, saying **կը** before your verb might lead to some confusion between referring to the present or referring to the future. And so, you add **կոր** to clear that up (Donabedian, 2018, p. 23).

There are many who don't consider the usage of **կոր** to be grammatically correct because of its overlap with Turkish -iyor but also either because of the sound of their sentences, or some socio-cultural perception of class and level of education associated with its usage (no formal sources but just from some online spaces I've encountered).

I've elected to keep the single English translation in present simple tense and opening the option to decide whether or not to use **կոր** when trying to express present continuous action. Even though it is not present in written literature, its usage is still prevalent in colloquial speech according to some of the Western Armenian speakers I have talked to and they believe it is important to acknowledge and so I will respect that.
